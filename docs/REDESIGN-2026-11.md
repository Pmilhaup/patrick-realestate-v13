**Data.** `public/data/history.json` is baked by `tools/sync_history.py` from the public view `mv_market_intelligence` (monthly rows through February 2026). Re-run it when a new settled month is loaded. The nightly tail comes from `daily_snapshots` in the browser.

**Map.** `tools/build_shore.mjs` projects the seven villages' Census boundaries with D3 Geo into `src/data/shore.json`; `CoastMap.astro` draws them and `site.js` shades them by the latest nightly figure.

**Open data question: months of supply.** The nightly table's `months_supply` and `homes_for_sale` do not match the monthly record. Lake Forest, October 2026: 46 homes for sale and 2.4 months in the monthly record; 113 homes and 8.1 months in the nightly table on 16 November. Closed sales and prices agree at the seam; inventory does not. Until that is reconciled the site shows days on market instead of supply wherever the figure is nightly, and the `history` chart does not extend supply past the monthly record.

**Page transitions.** Chrome sometimes skips the cross-page photographic transition (observed in headless test runs, more often while large images are decoding). The page then simply appears. The layout suppresses the resulting unhandled-rejection message.

**Performance and accessibility pass (commits `1ccf647`, `3e54a16`).**

- Intelligence: the embedded market map is `<iframe data-src="/market-map">`; `site.js` sets `src` when the frame is within 300 px of the viewport. Loading it with the page cost about 20 s of main-thread time on a simulated phone.
- Motion chart: moving marks sit on their own canvas layer (`zlevel: 1`), pixel ratio is capped at 1.5, and it autoplays once only on a wide screen with a fine pointer. On phones and under reduced motion it opens on the latest month and plays on request.
- Legacy pages (`body.is-legacy`): `--ink-4`, `--ink-5` and `--fg-fade` are lifted to `#8492AD` (5.6:1 on navy). The older style sheets set them at 4.1:1.
- Masthead link and property tiles carry visible text as their accessible name; no `aria-label` overrides on links.

Measured on the deployed preview, Lighthouse mobile: Home LCP 1.6 s (performance 99); `/sold/28-aberdeen-court` 1.5 s (100); Properties 2.2 s (99); Intelligence 1.7 s, blocking time 1.2 s (75). ## Fourth pass (17 Nov, night): home valuation, the Sotheby's library

**Home valuation (`/home-value`).** Six inputs return a three-part report computed in the browser: how much, when, why. Nothing typed leaves the page unless the visitor sends the CMA request at the end (`data-lead="valuation"`, which carries the inputs and the model's range in the message).

- *How much.* One log-linear regression per village on `v_cma_comp_pool`, 36 months: living area (with a squared term, which bends large homes the way the 6,000 sq ft rule does), lot, age and age squared, baths, new construction, renovated in the past ten years, and a time trend. Lakefront sales are excluded. The fit runs inside Postgres with a temporary solver function (`tools/sql/valuation_model.sql`); nothing persists in the database.
- *The range is measured, not assumed.* A second model is fitted on the 36 months before the last 12 and asked to price the last 12. The quartiles of actual / predicted in that blind test set the range shown; the 10th and 90th percentiles set the wider span. Median miss by village: Winnetka 12%, Kenilworth 12% (20 test sales), Lake Forest 13%, Glencoe 14%, Highland Park 17%, Wilmette 18%, Lake Bluff 21%. The page prints this table.
- *It declines.* Outside the 2nd to 98th percentile of what it was trained on (size, lot), outside the years or bath counts seen, or on the lake, it gives no number and routes to the CMA.
- *When.* `v_listing_lifecycle`, public launches 2021 to 2025 with a resolved outcome, by launch month: share that sold, median days to contract, median sale / first asking, share at or above first asking.
- *Why.* Closed sales launched since 2023 by days to contract: median sale / first asking and the share that took a price cut. Plus the count of sales near the estimate from `budget.json`, and the current month against the next strong month.
- Tried and not used: a finished-basement flag and a pool flag (coefficients near zero or unstable) and a one-kilometre location adjustment (blind-test median miss 15.3% to 13.9% overall; worth doing once an address can be geocoded).
- Data file: `public/data/valuation.json` (5 KB: coefficients, quantiles, monthly aggregates; no individual sale). Build: `tools/sql/valuation_model.sql`, `tools/sql/valuation_timing.sql`, `tools/build_valuation.py` with database checksums.
- The report prints as dark ink on white (print style sheet scoped to `body.page-value`).

**The offer, everywhere.** `ValueBand.astro` renders above the footer on every page except `/home-value` (layout prop `valueBand`; village guides pass `valueVillage`). It is a plain GET form, so it works without scripts; the tool reads `?v=` and `?sf=` and then clears them from the address bar. "Home value" is in the navigation; the full navigation now shows from 1240 px (was 1100) because it carries nine links. The home hero and Selling opening each gained a "What is my home worth?" action.

**The library (`/heritage#library`).** Four publisher links in the order Patrick sends them to a seller: the 2026 Mid-Year Luxury Outlook (luxuryoutlook.com), The Importance of Exposure, Property Marketing Strategy, and the Google Ads overview (presentation.jamesonps.com). ## Fifth pass (18 November 2026)

**Accuracy tiers on the valuation tool.** Two optional answers now sharpen the range, each with its own blind test (`tools/sql/valuation_tiers.sql`, baked by `tools/build_valuation.py` with checksums):

| Evidence | Typical miss, seven villages | Within 10% | Tested on |
|---|---|---|---|
| Records only | 15.1% | 34% | 817 sales |
| + last year's tax bill | 12.7% | 40% | 817 |
| + purchase price and year | 10.8% | 46% | 446 with a prior sale |
| + both | 10.1% | 50% | 446 |

With both, Lake Forest is 9.0%, Highland Park 9.2%, Glencoe 9.3%, Winnetka 10.3%, Lake Bluff 10.9%, Wilmette 12.0%. Rules in `src/scripts/value.js`: the tax bill is used only where it improved that village's own test (not Kenilworth, 18 sales), never for new construction (land-only bills), and only inside the 2nd to 98th percentile of bills the model saw. The purchase is used when it is 2008 or later and at least two years old; it is set aside when it lands more than 2.5x or less than 0.4x from the model (the house has changed). Purchase tiers draw their range from the pooled test, because no village has enough repeat sales in a year. What remains is condition, which no form can see.

**The CMA step.** The report now ends by offering the full CMA by email, and the request form follows the report directly. Address, name, email and phone are all required. The request carries the report's figures and a machine-readable block (`{v=...;sf=...;tax=...;paid=...;bought=...}`) inside `leads.message`.

**Delivery is a switch, not a promise.** `src/data/features.json` has `instantCma: false`. While false, the page says the CMA is sent to the visitor's email and that Patrick prepares it within the business day, which is what happens today: the request lands in `leads`. Set it to true only when the sender below is live; the page then says "generated and sent to your email within minutes". Do not merge with it true before that.

Design for the sender (not built, not deployed):
1. A database webhook on `leads` inserts with `source = 'valuation'` calls an edge function with a shared secret. Browsers never call the function, so comparable-sale data is not exposed to the public internet.
2. The function reads the request block, declines lakefront and out-of-range houses to a "prepared by hand" reply, selects comparables from `v_cma_comp_pool` by the CMA v2 method, and renders the Paper Edition report.
3. It emails the owner through the existing transactional mail service from Patrick's address, copies Patrick, and writes the outcome to `leads.status`. The mail key is a function secret that Patrick sets; it is never in this repository.
4. Illinois requires a broker price opinion or CMA to be in writing with seven items (225 ILCS 454/10-45): purpose, the interest valued, method, assumptions and limits, any interest of the broker, the broker's name, license number and signature, and the statutory not-an-appraisal statement. The template carries all seven.
5. Limits: one report per address per day, a cap per email, and a daily total.
